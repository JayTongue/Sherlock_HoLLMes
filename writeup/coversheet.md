\noindent
{\large\bfseries Abstract}

\vspace{0.04in}

\begin{footnotesize}

\setlength{\parindent}{0pt}
\setlength{\parskip}{0.28em}

Legal technology vendors offer products with different capabilities. One capability often offered is the ability to upload documents as a custom database and query it using an AI chat function. This study presents an experiment, data, and analysis of the performance of legal AI tools from Westlaw, Lexis, and Harvey when used with different types and quantities of uploaded text.

I create specified sets of files and seed them with ``clues'' corresponding to questions posed to each legal AI tool across a range of retrieval and logic tasks. Each response is evaluated according to the tool's ability to retrieve the seeded information.

The analysis describes the relationship between legal AI performance, file-set size, and corpus type, with particular attention to retrieval capability and accuracy. The results indicate correlations between file-set size and performance and, for all tested products, between corpus type and performance. This research provides data that users of legal AI tools can use to better understand these systems and make informed decisions about their use.

\end{footnotesize}
